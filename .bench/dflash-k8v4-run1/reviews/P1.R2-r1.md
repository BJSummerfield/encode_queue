### 7. Prescriptive points the notes missed (searched the full prose, sections 23–1781)
Found several; the two most significant:
1. **The hard-dependency decision test** (raw §"Hard dependencies and hexagonal architecture:
   how to make the right call"): the article explicitly allows *some* hard dependencies to flow
   freely — Tokio (fundamental async runtime) and `anyhow` ("allowed to flow freely through many
   Rust apps") — while prescribing that DB clients/HTTP packages/message queues must be
   abstracted, and that when choosing a hard dependency you should "look for evidence of
   widespread community adoption and support" and accept that switching later is a painful
   refactor. The notes never state this test or the Tokio/anyhow carve-outs.
2. **Project structure convention + documentation** (raw §"A Rust project template for
   hexagonal architecture"): the article prescribes splitting `src/lib` into `domain` /
   `inbound` / `outbound` (the layout S29's `use` statements actually show), notes it "isn't
   sacred" but has worked at scale, and insists: "whatever convention you adopt, you document
   it… Document your decisions, _I beg you_." The notes say nothing about module layout or
   documenting architectural decisions.
Additional missed points (worth noting): master-record/auth guidance (§"Authentication and
authorization with hexagonal architecture" — `User` + auth in its own domain with unique owner
references for larger/third-party-auth apps; "at a certain scale, `User` deletion can't be
modeled atomically"), the adoption-fit criteria (§"Is hexagonal architecture right for you?" —
skip for solo/head-sized apps and apps with no business logic; build hexagonally from day one
for startups scaling hard; "If you can't build a modular monolith, you're not qualified to run
microservices"), and the notification-medium judgement call ("Finding the right abstraction"
aside: the domain may legitimately need to know the medium, e.g. a hard email requirement).

## Findings

1. [minor] `notes.md` R2 states "Never let a concrete third-party type flow through the whole
   application", which overstates the article's position: the article explicitly permits
   `anyhow` to "flow freely through many Rust apps" and treats the async runtime (Tokio) as a
   legitimate, unavoidable hard dependency, and the article's own `CreateAuthorError::Unknown`
   wraps `anyhow::Error` across the port boundary (S9/S19). Fix: soften R2 to "never let
   swappable concrete types (DB clients, HTTP servers, message queues) flow through the
   application" and add the article's carve-outs (fundamental runtime + widely adopted crates
   like `anyhow`), citing the §"Hard dependencies…" prose.
2. [minor] R8's catch-all claim ("the adapter adds an `Unknown`-style variant wrapping
   `anyhow::Error` … (S6, S9)") is supported more directly by S19 (whose code shows
   `CreateAuthorError::Unknown(cause)` and `cause.backtrace()`); S6 only shows the `Duplicate`
   variant. Fix: cite S19 alongside S6/S9 in R8.
3. [minor] Coverage gaps (see §7 above): no rule captures (a) the hard-dependency decision
   test/Tokio/anyhow carve-outs, (b) the `domain`/`inbound`/`outbound` project layout +
